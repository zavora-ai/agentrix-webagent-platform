How a mission starts

Say the goal, read the contract, then sign it

01 · You say it
"Get us 60 Christmas cake preorders by 18 December. Don't spend more than KSh 400 on it."

By typing, by voice, or on WhatsApp.

02 · The Chief WebAgent compiles it

A contract you can read: what counts, who works on it, what wakes them, the budget, the end date. It reuses each WebAgent's current authority; it can't add any. If something is unclear it asks you one question.

03 · You sign, it runs

You approve it; because this one adds a new measure to your charter, your passkey signs it (or, without one, a code from your authenticator app approves it). The WebAgents wake on the mission's schedule and on its events, work mostly at night, and report every Monday. Pause or end it with one tap.

Honest reporting

Reports are written from the ledger, the agenda and the stores, and each claim links to its source. The counting method is shown, including what it misses. "Didn't work" is a section, not an afterthought.

A mission never raises a WebAgent's authority

A mission bundles goals, wakes and a budget. It never grants authority: each step runs at the level of the job that does it. At the budget cap the mission stops and asks.

Built on the kernel

Under the hood a mission is a goal on the charter, agenda items, the jobs' standing wakes and a KPI tracker, tied together and shown as one thing. It survives restarts and crashes because its processes do.
