Design language

How a WebAgent shows it is working

A mark, in the site's colours

A WebAgent is a small mark in the host brand with its menu name. It never has a face, a human name, typing dots or a pop-up greeting. Its state is always true.

The page is the avatar

When a WebAgent cites a price, the price row on the real page lights up. Tours scroll and highlight the actual sections. Motion belongs to the content, not to a character.

WidgetA bubble in the corner that opens itself and forgets you on the next page.
WebAgentA menu you can talk to, one thread across pages, answers that point at the page.

Static stays instant

Pages are static, signed releases. The agent layer loads after the page is usable. Hover is free, with no model call. Everything works without JavaScript or without a model, and says so honestly when resting.

Answer first, chat second. The page and search answer; conversation is there when wanted.
The business defines the role. Answer, guide, make, present. No companionship, no edgy modes.
Plainly AI, never a person. AI is disclosed at first contact, the WebAgent has a role name, and a real person is one tap away with an honest wait time.
Every claim points to the page. Citations land on the cited element. "The site doesn't say" is a feature.
Models compose, rules decide. Prices, dates, stock and refunds come from rules and the release, never from a model.
Memory is a ledger the visitor holds. Session by default. Device or account memory is opt-in, visible and deletable.
Confirm before you remember. A passing interest is never silently turned into a preference.
Proactive only on task. One relevant, dismissible offer. No streaks, nudges or re-engagement pushes.
Honest, not flattering. "Over budget", "not offered" and "I can't confirm" are said plainly.
Owner's authority, visible. Visitors see what waits for the owner and why.

What we will not do

Full list and reasons in docs/06-website-experience.md §10
  • Pop-up chat, greetings on arrival, or agents that open themselves
  • Human names, faces or fake typing for AI
  • Companion, romantic or engagement-maximising features
  • Prices, dates, stock or refunds stated by a model
  • Generated images presented as the product
  • Cross-site profiles, fingerprinting or training on visitor data
  • AI-written news, or paraphrased subscriber content
  • False urgency, fake scarcity or dark patterns
  • A site that stops working when the model does
  • Agents that decide eligibility, diagnose, or promise refunds
