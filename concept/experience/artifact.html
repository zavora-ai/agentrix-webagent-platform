Experience 04 · Website artifacts

Plans you make with a desk and share with friends

An artifact is a document a visitor and a WebAgent make together on the site: a gift plan, a cake brief, a quote, a comparison, an itinerary. It has its own private link. Friends can open it, choose and ask questions, each within the permissions they were given. When it's ready, it becomes one request the owner confirms. Prices are pinned to the release they came from.

What an artifact is

  • A typed document (brief, plan, quote, comparison, itinerary, deck) made of safe blocks. No raw HTML, uploads or outside links, so it can't be used for phishing.
  • Its own link, never indexed. Visibility is private, link or named people. Each person gets a permission: view, comment, choose or request.
  • Facts pinned to a release, like a quote: prices hold until a date, and changed facts are flagged.
  • Visitor-written blocks are marked and treated as untrusted when a WebAgent reads them.

What the owner gets

  • One plan turns five separate decisions into one order.
  • Every shared link carries the shop's brand into a group chat: word of mouth with a working "buy" inside.
  • The owner sees the plan only if the visitor allows it. Back-office WebAgents get only typed fields.
  • Artifacts expire after 30 days unless saved.

Artifacts never

  • Become public pages or appear in search engines
  • Let a recipient order or pay unless the creator allowed it
  • Show a recipient the prices if the creator hid them
  • Let text someone typed change what the WebAgent may do
