How your WebAgents work

Three kinds of conversation, one team.

Your customers and your WebAgents

Visitors ask your site's desks anything, at any hour. Each desk says it's AI, answers from your own pages, shows the page it used, and passes anything that needs a person to you.

You and your WebAgents

Your Chief WebAgent briefs you each morning. The others ask before anything that costs money or makes a promise, and you answer with one tap on your phone. You can read any conversation and take it over.

Other AI agents and your WebAgents

A customer's AI assistant or agent comes in through the agent door. Your site says what agents may do, over AWP and MCP, and an order from an agent waits for your OK.

The Agentic Web Protocol

One website, two interfaces.

More of the web is now read by AI assistants acting for people. An Agentrix Website serves both: pages for people, and a structured interface for AI agents, built on ADK-Rust's Agentic Web Protocol (AWP).

For people

A clear website, conversations with your WebAgent desks, forms and approvals, as people expect.

For AI agents

A manifest at /.well-known/awp that says what your business offers, what's current, what an agent may do and when it must ask you.

One set of facts

Both read the same published facts, prices and policies, so an agent never has to guess what a person would see.

Inspect this site's agent manifest · How AWP works, on adk-rust.com

The technology

Built on the ADK-Rust kernel.

Agentrix is written in Rust on ADK-Rust, Zavora's open-source agent development kit. Its kernel lets each WebAgent work on its own while you stay in charge.

One job each

Every WebAgent has one job, its own tools and a list of things it never does without you.

Dials, not blind trust

Each job has a dial for how much it may do alone. Only you turn a dial up, and money and promises always ask first.

A record you can check

Every decision you approve is signed and kept as a receipt you can open and verify.

Open doors for agents

Other agents reach your site over AWP and MCP, so it works with the assistants your customers already use.

The model writes code

A model writes each page of the new site. A sealed room builds and renders it with no network. Errors go back to the model, up to three tries.

A browser in a room

A browser reads the old site inside a room, and a vision model compares old and new page by page. A page below 95% waits for a person.

Containment

Nothing the old site tries gets out.

  • Process isolation

    A separate VX1 machine. Each job runs in a gVisor room: read-only, not root, with no capabilities.

  • No secrets in the sandbox

    No keys on the sandbox machine. The proxy refuses the cloud's metadata address.

  • Hard limits

    Caps on CPU, memory, processes and time for every job.

  • Rooms are destroyed

    Every room is destroyed after its job, and the signed receipt says so.

Go live

Live only on the owner's say-so.

The passkey signs this card.

Its challenge commits to the card's fingerprint: the release's digest and the site's charter. Without a passkey, an authenticator code is bound to the same fingerprint.

Only then is the release signed.

The edge serves signed releases only. A release that fails its check is refused, and the last good one stays up.

The WebAgents

Nine WebAgents, each with one job.

Each WebAgent has one job, a budget, and a dial for what it may do without asking. Each wears a living mark in its site's own colours, which moves only while it works. Visitors always see when they're talking to one.

  • Chief WebAgentBriefs you and routes decisions
  • ConciergeThe front desk, day and night
  • SalesLeads, quotes and bookings
  • EditorKeeps every page up to date
  • ReputationDrafts replies to reviews for you to approve
  • CashierOrders and payments
  • GuardianWatches the site and can roll it back
  • CaretakerDomains, backups and costs
  • MoverThe Move, then hands over
